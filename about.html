About

Curating the hours

The Earthly Book of Hours — Horae Terrenae in Latin — is a daily primer of art, literature, and ecology (a primer can be a devotional object or an introductory learning tool). This contemplative archive of images, readings, and natural phenomena is neither a mystical text nor a self-help manual, but something in between — a portiforium poeticum or, perhaps, a modernist breviary. It is designed for encountering the day (or night) and yourself in it, for observing biological and cultural rhythms: seeing inside or under or through. Use it to navigate the nychthemeron and chart your chronotype.

The book is arranged in three sections, each introduced by its own incipit: The Hours, a poetic rendition of the canonical hours of medieval books of hours; The Seasons, home to Lectio Terra, an annual cycle of species readings; and The Weather, an oracular weather service.

The Sources page provides documentation of the readings and images included in The Earthly Book of Hours, along with links to recommended editions, freely available texts, and contextual essays for further reading.

The materials gathered here are drawn primarily from the public domain, representing works whose passage into common inheritance makes this kind of curation possible. Any works subject to copyright restrictions are included in the spirit of fair use: for educational, illustrative, and non-commercial purposes, in the belief that brief encounter leads to deeper reading. Some links to books and editions across the site are provided through Bookshop.org; any small commission supports independent booksellers and helps sustain this project. If you are a rights holder with a concern about a specific work, please be in touch.

This site is an ongoing project. If you have suggestions for quotes, images, or natural phenomena to include, or if you'd like to follow along with this work and related activities, subscribe to Lectio Terra on Substack or visit my space, Otherwise, on DFOS.

Colophon

The Earthly Book of Hours is set in open-source typefaces, hosted here alongside their licenses.

Titles are set in GL-Morris, Gutenberg Labo's digitization of the Troy Type that William Morris designed for the Kelmscott Press in the early 1890s. The readings and small capitals are set in IM Fell English, Igino Marini's revival of the Fell Types, gathered by Bishop John Fell for the press at Oxford in the 1670s and probably cut by Christoffel van Dijck, Robert Granjon and Peter de Walpergen. My own writing appears in Fraunces, designed by Phaedra Charles and Flavia Zimbardi of Undercase Type (2020), and citations and captions in Cormorant Garamond, Christian Thalmann's 2015 reimagining of Claude Garamond's sixteenth-century romans.

None of these typefaces had a star of its own, so the four-pointed one that punctuates these pages (✦) was drawn for the site.
